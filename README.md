# U.S. Highway Construction Cost Analysis — NHCCI (2003–2025)

## Project Overview

This project analyses **22 years (91 quarters)** of U.S. National Highway Construction Cost Index (NHCCI) data published by the **Federal Highway Administration (FHWA), U.S. Department of Transportation**.

The goal is to identify long-run cost trends, quantify major inflation events, and pinpoint which construction segments drove cost volatility — replicating the kind of secondary research and structured data reporting used in global construction industry intelligence.


## Research Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| Q1 | How have U.S. highway construction costs trended from 2003 to 2025? |
| Q2 | What were the major cost shock events and how severe were they? |
| Q3 | Which construction segments drove the post-COVID inflation surge? |
| Q4 | Is there evidence of cost stabilisation in 2024–2025? |

---

##  Charts & Key Findings

### Chart 1 — Long-Term NHCCI Trend (2003–2025)
> Costs rose **+218% cumulatively** over 22 years. A project costing $1M in 2003 requires ~$3.2M today. Three phases visible: 2003–08 growth, 2009–19 stable decade, 2021–24 inflation surge.

### Chart 2 — Year-on-Year % Change
> **2022 recorded the sharpest spike in 22 years at +26.3%** — more than double the previous worst year. The 2009 GFC (−12.9%) remains the only sustained cost reversal in the dataset.

### Chart 3 — Segment Cost Heatmap (2018–2025)
> **Asphalt surged +251% in 2022** (crude oil dependency), **Bridge +85%** (steel prices). Grading/Excavation stayed elevated 3+ years. Electrical is rising again in 2025 (+19%) — an emerging risk.

### Chart 4 — Raw vs Seasonally Adjusted (2019–2025)
> Both lines flatten together in 2024–2025, confirming the plateau is **structural, not seasonal**. Costs have stabilised at a permanently higher level (~3.2× the 2003 baseline).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Highway construction costs have **tripled since 2003** — infrastructure budgets anchored to pre-2021 estimates are structurally underfunded
- The **2022 inflation shock was historically exceptional**, driven simultaneously by oil, steel, supply chain disruption, and labour shortages
- **Segment forecasting requires commodity tracking** — asphalt follows oil, bridges follow steel, excavation follows diesel
- **Electrical costs are the emerging risk in 2025–2026**, driven by EV charging and smart highway infrastructure demand
- Costs are now stabilising but at a **permanently higher level** — this is the new baseline for project budgeting
